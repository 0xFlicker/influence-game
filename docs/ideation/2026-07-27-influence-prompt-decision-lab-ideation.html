Grounding Context

Codebase Context

The repo is much closer to supporting this lab than the current fixture harness makes it look. The hard missing piece is not token counting; it is a trustworthy experiment boundary that binds real state, context selection, provider execution, and review.

What already exists

  • Canonical events and projections for authoritative game state.
  • Ordered transcripts, actor continuity, phase-boundary checkpoints, and recovery evidence.
  • Private call traces with prompt/request/response, usage, model, action, and output evidence.
  • RecallPlan receipts, reusable-prefix estimates, provider cached-token usage, and cost rollups.
  • Hosted OpenAI, Katana, LM Studio, and custom OpenAI-compatible provider lanes.

What is actually missing

  • A case materializer that selects one real decision or short thread from DB/storage.
  • A versioned prompt/context policy stack that baseline and candidate can swap cleanly.
  • A deterministic runway from a supported checkpoint to the call being compared.
  • A persistent experiment identity joining state, outputs, repetitions, cache, spend, and review.
  • A producer workbench that makes those differences legible.

What the current harness is

The uncommitted prompt-scenario-lab is a structural unit-test seam: hand-built snapshot, two action adapters, fake provider, fresh agent, no DB selector, no model output, no actual spend, and no ordered conversation. Its fresh-agent reuse result cannot validate cache behavior.

What quality can mean

Quality is not one score. The lab can prove canonical and structured validity, retrieval coverage, fallbacks, budget adherence, and cache accounting. It can estimate behavior through repetitions and calibrated model graders. Strategy, persuasion, promise-following, novelty, and watchability still require blinded human judgment.

DB or fixtures?

Both, with a hard distinction. Real DB/private storage is the case source. The lab then materializes an immutable, provenance-rich experiment snapshot so the same case can be rerun safely and cheaply. Hand-authored synthetic fixtures stay in unit tests; they do not count as product-level evidence.

External grounding
  • OpenAI prompt caching — exact-prefix behavior, cache keys, retention, and usage fields.
  • OpenAI evaluation best practices — pairwise grading, human calibration, retrieval metrics, and nondeterminism.
  • Braintrust evaluations — immutable experiments, cases, scorers, traces, and comparisons.
  • LangSmith repetitions — repeated trials and dispersion.
  • Ragas metrics — separation of retrieval and response-quality evaluation.
  • Promptfoo conversations — generic multi-turn replay prior art.

Real-game case corpus and replay fidelity

1. Influence Decision Workbench

The coherent product: select an immutable production decision episode or short social thread, choose baseline and candidate policy stacks, inspect the state/history/RecallPlan/request delta, run the cheapest provider panel that can answer the question, and review all evidence in one experiment notebook. The workbench is backed by real DB/storage cases but runs on lab-owned immutable snapshots; it never appends production events.

Illustrative product loop: one immutable case identity binds every comparison and review artifact.

Axis
Real-game case corpus and replay fidelity
Basis
direct: canonical events rebuild board state while traces and turns explain model attempts; the repo already stores request, usage, reuse, and cost evidence but has no bound comparison object (CONCEPTS.md:342-350).
Rationale
This defines the end state clearly and prevents six disconnected utilities from recreating the manual stitching the lab is supposed to remove.
Downsides
High product and architecture scope. Read-only authority, case provenance, replay fidelity, and immutable experiment identity are non-negotiable or this becomes expensive theater.

2

Real-game case corpus and replay fidelity

2. Replay Fidelity Contract

Every case declares one of three honest capabilities: exact pre-render capture, deterministic checkpoint-to-call reconstruction, or request-only observation. Experiments may compare rendered historical requests in all three classes, but only the first two can support rerendering a different context policy against the same state; every conclusion is visibly capped by the case’s fidelity.

Axis
Real-game case corpus and replay fidelity
Basis
direct: checkpoints are phase-boundary evidence while private traces separately preserve rendered prompt/request/response artifacts (packages/api/src/db/schema.ts:1204-1221; packages/engine/src/game-runner.types.ts:460-509).
Rationale
A lab is trustworthy only if it says what it actually replayed. Existing games still provide valuable observation cases while future capture earns stronger counterfactual claims.
Downsides
Many historical calls will initially be observation-only. Stronger comparisons require pre-render capture or a verified deterministic runway from a checkpoint.

3

Relevant-history selection and prompt/context variant comparison

3. Differential Context Compiler

Treat authoritative game state and eligible history as source, RecallPlan as an intermediate representation, and the provider request as emitted output. The workbench compares baseline and candidate across all three layers: included and omitted evidence, selection reason and rank, protected facts, ordering, rendered blocks, prefix stability, token burden, and the resulting decision. A prompt that is smaller but forgets the decisive promise loses.

Illustrative compiler view: context optimization has semantic output, not just byte output.

Axis
Relevant-history selection and prompt/context variant comparison
Basis
direct: ContextBuilder attaches a compiled RecallPlan, while the current harness reports mostly size, count, and overflow deltas (packages/engine/src/context-builder.ts:848-859; packages/engine/src/prompt-scenario-lab.ts:278-287).
Rationale
This directly answers the question the user cares about: did the candidate select the right history, and did that different evidence plausibly improve the decision?
Downsides
A decision change is correlated with the context delta, not automatically causal. Targeted ablations and repetitions are still necessary.

95%

4

Threaded conversations and prompt-cache reuse

4. Threaded Cache Wind Tunnel

Replay one authentic three-to-four-turn multi-agent Mingle or huddle tape with the original turn order and repeated calls by the same actor. Baseline and candidate see the same starting state and conversation growth. Each turn reports structural reusable-prefix potential, provider cache reads and writes, latency, and actual cost; an early-prefix mutation and a late-conversation mutation prove the measurement reacts for the expected reason.

Illustrative cache tape: social continuity and cache economics are measured on the same ordered thread, but remain separate claims.

Axis
Threaded conversations and prompt-cache reuse
Basis
direct: Mingle accumulates ordered conversation history and the docs require ordered same-agent replay before interpreting reuse (packages/engine/src/phases/mingle.ts:583-617,642-652; docs/reasoning-transcript-observability.md:337). Structural reuse and provider cached tokens already exist as separate signals.
Rationale
This is the smallest bounded experiment that still behaves like Influence social gameplay and can validate real OpenAI cache economics.
Downsides
Real cache validation requires hosted calls within the provider’s cache window. Local models can test behavior, not OpenAI billing.

5

Provider panels, spend governance, and experiment repeatability

5. Evidence-per-Dollar Experiment Ladder

Every experiment states the question it is trying to answer. Structural and retrieval checks run first, selected local 9B–31B models run second, and targeted hosted repetitions run only when cheaper evidence leaves a meaningful disagreement. An immutable receipt locks case, variant, actor/runtime snapshot, model/provider policy, repetition, effective service tier, usage, and cost provenance so a result cannot quietly become apples-to-oranges.

Illustrative escalation: spend is justified by the unresolved question, not by a default model matrix.

Axis
Provider panels, spend governance, and experiment repeatability
Basis
direct: the provider catalog already spans hosted OpenAI, Katana, LM Studio, and custom-compatible models; accounting already records provider, model, token, tier, and cost provenance (packages/engine/src/model-catalog.ts:245-270; packages/api/src/services/provider-cost-accounting.ts:490-568).
Rationale
The user can spend more on decisive evidence while spending nothing on variants that already failed structural or retrieval checks.
Downsides
Seed/repetition support and pre-run budget enforcement are not fully established. Local-model behavior is screening evidence, not a proxy for hosted behavior.

Behavioral quality evidence and review

6. Quality Claim Ledger and Blind Strategy Jury

The lab keeps separate verdict lanes for canonical and structured validity, relevant-history retrieval, fallback provenance, cache and cost, repetition stability, model-grader triage, and blinded human preference. Humans see baseline and candidate decisions without model labels, with canonical exhibits beside them, and judge promise-following, strategic coherence, persuasion, novelty, repetition, and watchability. The lab never emits one universal quality number.

Axis
Behavioral quality evidence and review
Basis
direct: reasoning artifacts explain attempted action but are not canonical game facts, while the existing human rubric already asks about good and bad strategy, pivots, coherence, repetition, and watchability (docs/local-model-evaluation.md:262-276).
Rationale
This makes quality evaluation possible and useful without pretending subjective social strategy is a deterministic unit test.
Downsides
Human labels are sparse and may disagree. Model judges must be calibrated against them, used mainly for triage, and never presented as final truth.
