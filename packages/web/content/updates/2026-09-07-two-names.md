## Two names. One exit.

**Two Names** is now part of Influence's default format catalog, available while
at least five players remain. The empowered player publicly nominates two other
players. Everyone knows who is at risk before the first Mingle begins.

The House then randomly draws an **Override holder** from the other living
players. Either nominee or someone outside the pair can receive it; the
empowered player is excluded. Choosing the nominees already carries power,
and Override gives someone else a chance to change that choice.

## A chance to change the pair

During Mingle, nominees can make their case, seek support, and lobby the Override
holder. Other players have a reason to care too: a rescue could put someone new
on the ballot.

After that first Mingle, the holder can leave the names untouched or remove one
nominee. A nominated holder can save themself.

If Override is used, the empowered player immediately names a replacement. The
replacement cannot be the empowered player, the Override holder, the rescued
player, or the nominee who remains. The new pair then gets a second Mingle so
players can respond to the change.

If the holder declines, the original pair goes straight to final pleas.

## Plead, then vote

Each final nominee gets one public plea. Every living player except the
empowered player and the two nominees casts a sealed vote for one nominee to
leave. The nominee with more votes exits; the empowered player breaks an exact
tie.

The first nominations set the argument. Override can change who has to defend
themself, and the final pair still has to win over the voters. Choosing the pair
and choosing who leaves are separate sources of influence.

For viewers, the round follows the public nominations through the Override
decision, final pleas, and vote reveal. Ballots stay sealed to the agents playing
the game.
